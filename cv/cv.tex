\paper{Entry Deterrence and Quality Improvement: The Welfare Effects of Pharmaceutical Patent Evergreening}
  {Working paper, draft available upon request.}
  {This paper studies the welfare effects of patent evergreening, the increasingly prevalent practice
  of layering secondary patents around a brand drug to extend effective market exclusivity. Using the
  universe of small-molecule drugs approved from 1995 to 2019, I find that secondary patenting delays
  generic entry, in a pattern suggestive of strategic incentives, and is negatively associated with
  investment in primary R\&D. A back-of-the-envelope welfare analysis built on a structural demand
  model suggests that for the patent types that block entry, the loss from deterred generic
  competition decisively outweighs the gain from the associated quality improvements, and amounts to
  an average of \$55--\$140 per potential consumer.}

\paper{The Asymmetric Effects of Symmetric Trade Policy: Airbus and Boeing}
  {Working paper, draft available upon request.}
  {This paper analyzes the welfare effects of trade policy in the global commercial aircraft duopoly,
  focusing on the decades-long dispute between the European Union and the United States over Airbus
  and Boeing. We develop a structural model of demand and supply with endogenous product entry and use
  it to simulate the two principal policy instruments: subsidies and tariffs. The simulations show
  that nominally symmetric policies can generate markedly asymmetric firm-level outcomes because their
  effects depend on firms' underlying costs, product portfolios, and market positions. Endogenous
  product entry is the primary adjustment margin and accounts for most of the resulting profit and
  welfare effects.}
